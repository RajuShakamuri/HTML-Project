Cricket

Cricket is played on an oval field with a rectangular pitch at its center, measuring 22 yards long. Each team takes turns batting and fielding. The batting team aims to score runs by hitting the ball and running between two sets of wickets, while the fielding team tries to dismiss the batters and limit runs scored. Dismissals can occur in various ways, including being bowled, caught, or run out. An innings ends when ten batters are out, and the teams switch roles. Wikipedia +1 Formats of Cricket Cricket has several formats, including: Test Matches: Played over five days, allowing two innings per team. One Day Internationals (ODIs): Each team bats for a maximum of 50 overs. Twenty20 (T20): Each team bats for 20 overs, making it a fast-paced format. 2 2 Sources Historical Context Cricket's origins can be traced back to the 16th century in England, evolving from a children's game to a major sport. The first recorded Test match took place in 1877 between Australia and England. The Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC), established in 1787, is responsible for maintaining the Laws of Cricket. Britannica +1 Cultural Significance Cricket is particularly popular in countries like India, Australia, England, and South Africa, often regarded as a cultural phenomenon. The sport has seen significant growth in women's cricket, especially in the 21st century, reflecting broader societal changes. Wikipedia +1 Governing Body The International Cricket Council (ICC) oversees international cricket, with over 100 member nations. The ICC organizes major tournaments, including the Cricket World Cup and T20 World Cup, which attract global audiences.
